Why Accountants and Store Managers Often Disagree About Stock

ASOct 6, 20264 min read

In many businesses, the accountant and the store manager look at stock from very different angles. This difference in perspective often leads to conflict.

A familiar exchange

"The system says we have 100 units."
"But there are only 85 on the shelf."

It's a familiar conversation in many businesses. Before either team blames the other, it helps to understand what each is tracking.

The Accountant Follows the Records

Accountants need to know what stock the business owns, what it cost, and how its value affects financial reporting. They rely on documented purchases, sales, returns, and adjustments.

When goods arrive before the paperwork, or stock leaves without a recorded transaction, the records can fall behind.

The Store Manager Follows the Movement

Store managers focus on what is physically on the shelves and whether it can be issued or sold. They handle partial deliveries, damaged goods, customer returns, and internal requests. During a busy day, stock can move faster than the paperwork.

The Gap Grows When Movements Go Unrecorded

Common causes include:

  • Goods received or issued without prompt system updates.
  • Returns, damages, and internal use left undocumented.
  • Inconsistent units, such as buying in cartons and issuing in pieces.
  • Separate spreadsheets or manual records.
  • Unclear responsibility for investigating discrepancies.

Agreement Starts with a Shared Process

Every stock movement needs a record. Both teams need consistent units, clear procedures, and regular physical counts followed by reconciliation.

An integrated ERP system like Acumatica can give accounts and stores a shared view of stock, but its accuracy depends on timely entries and reliable processes.

The Goal Is Simple

The stock on the shelf, the quantity in the system, and the value in the accounts should tell the same story.
