ERP Tip: Support, Not Surveillance

ASSep 1, 20265 min read

ERP adoption succeeds when it's introduced as a tool for support—not surveillance.

When employees hear "Now we can spot bottlenecks, remove unnecessary work, and give better support" they feel empowered. When they hear "Now we'll know who causes problems" they resist.

Key Principle

Your ERP should simplify work, not complicate trust.

Why ERP Can Feel Sinister

Employees may resist ERP because it:

  • Makes individual actions, delays, and mistakes permanently visible.
  • Removes informal shortcuts and personal discretion.
  • Exposes skill gaps or hidden process problems.
  • Demands accurate data entry.
  • May be linked to restructuring, automation, or job losses.

How to Position ERP as a Work Simplifier

For employees to embrace ERP, management must demonstrate personal benefits:

  • Fewer repeated reports
  • Faster approvals
  • Less paperwork
  • Fewer errors
  • Clearer responsibilities

Leaders must also explain how data will—and will not—be used and correct bad processes.

Case in Point: Finance Manager Resistance

One organisation discovered that enthusiasm during ERP training did not translate into ownership after go-live. The Finance Manager continued using QuickBooks, undermining ERP adoption.

Possible reasons included:

  • Lack of confidence in performing live transactions.
  • Fear of blame if migrated balances were incorrect.
  • Incomplete or disputed opening balances.
  • Overwhelm from migration workload plus normal duties.
  • Passive resistance to ERP visibility and discipline.
Lesson

Training ≠ adoption. Interest ≠ ownership. Test transactions ≠ consistent live use.

Why This Matters

Finance is central to ERP. If bills, payments, and balances remain outside the system:

  • Management cannot rely on ERP financial reports.
  • Supplier balances and purchasing may be inaccurate.
  • Duplicate or missed payments become possible.
  • The company risks running two conflicting sources of truth.
  • Audit trails and internal controls are compromised.

After a month, this is no longer a training issue—it's an operational and governance issue.

What Management Should Do

Hold a direct, fact-based meeting. Require specific answers:

  1. Which finance activities remain outside ERP?
  2. What has prevented completion?
  3. Are obstacles technical, data-related, workload-related, or behavioural?
  4. Is QuickBooks still being used, and under whose authorisation?
  5. What assistance is genuinely required?
  6. What exact dates will balances and payments be migrated?

Then agree on a written recovery plan with short deadlines:

  • Validate opening balances.
  • Enter outstanding supplier bills.
  • Record and reconcile payments.
  • Reconcile bank, supplier, and customer accounts.
  • Generate trial balance and finance reports.
  • Formally establish ERP as the financial source of truth.

Provide support where genuine problems exist, but don't let "more training" become an indefinite substitute for performance.

The Strongest Lesson

An ERP champion should be chosen based on ownership, reliability, and post go-live behaviour—not enthusiasm during training alone.
